Chapter 5: The Unseen Current

Kael blinked. Not just a habitual clearing of his vision, but a slow, heavy blink that felt like prying open leaden shutters. The manufactured hum of Aethelburg was still there, a constant companion, but now it felt different. Thicker. It wasn't just in the walls, the floor, his bones. It was in his head, a faint, insistent thrum that resonated behind his eyes, a low-frequency hum that seemed to *vibrate* his thoughts. He squeezed his eyes shut, then opened them.

His small, sterile hab-unit, a cube of grey synth-steel and recycled air, pulsed with the city’s omnipresent drone. But now, Kael perceived a new layer beneath the familiar. It was as if the hum had found a channel directly into his awareness, bypassing his ears, resonating deep within his skull. He pressed the heels of his hands against his temples, a futile gesture against an internal tremor. The thrum intensified, making his teeth ache.

His vision swam for a moment, the sharp edges of his utilitarian cot and the dull glow of his dataplate blurring. He felt a dizzying pressure, like standing at the edge of a great precipice, the air thin and charged. He focused on the flickering numeral on his chronometer – 04:37 – the designated time for sector-wide re-pressurization cycle, a daily inconvenience. This wasn't that. This was… internal.

He drew a shaky breath, the recycled air suddenly tasting metallic, sharp. He reached for the water ration cube on his bedside shelf, his fingers fumbling. As his hand brushed against it, a faint blue spark, no bigger than a pinprick, leaped from his fingertip to the cube's edge. He recoiled, gasping. The cube clattered to the floor, spilling its precious contents across the synth-mat.

Panic flared, cold and sharp. Spilled water was a sanitation violation, a mark against his daily compliance rating. But the spark… that wasn't a static discharge. He’d worked in the electro-processing plant for three cycles. He knew static. This was different. A contained, concentrated burst.

He knelt, swiping at the spreading dampness with a sleeve, his heart hammering against his ribs. The hum in his head pulsed in time with it. As he focused on the water, willing it away, willing the mess to vanish, he felt a strange pressure build behind his eyes, a surge of that unseen current. And then, impossibly, a tendril of the spilled liquid, barely a droplet, seemed to shudder, then recoiled, like a tiny frightened worm, towards the intact portion of the cube. It didn't fully absorb, but it moved. It *responded*.

Kael froze, his hand hovering inches from the synth-mat. His breath hitched. Had he imagined it? His sleep-deprived mind, stressed by work, by the unrelenting pressure of Aethelburg’s scrutiny… yes, that had to be it. A trick of the light, a spasm in his vision.

He squeezed his eyes shut again, focusing on the hum. It was still there, a low, vibrant drone, now seeming to ebb and flow with his concentration. He felt a connection to it, like a tuning fork vibrating in response to an unseen wave. He slowly opened his eyes, fixing them on the fallen water cube.

"No," he whispered, his voice hoarse. "This isn't real."

But the hum intensified, a silent roar in his head. He concentrated, willing the water to move again. He pictured it. He *felt* the connection, a faint thread of energy extending from his mind, through his hands, towards the liquid. Nothing happened for a moment. Then, with a sudden, unnerving jerk, the remaining water on the floor shivered, then scattered into a dozen tiny beads, rolling erratically across the synth-mat before settling. It wasn't a clean sweep, not a perfect control, but it was undeniable movement. Movement *he* had caused.

A cold sweat broke out on his brow. His abilities. The word felt alien, dangerous. He was an anomaly. In Aethelburg, anomalies were reprocessed. They disappeared. He knew the stories, the hushed whispers about those who deviated, those who couldn't conform.

He scrambled to his feet, heart still pounding. He had to calm down, rationalize this. He paced the tiny hab-unit, three steps to the re-fresher unit, three steps back to the synth-mat. The hum followed him, a constant, vibrating echo of his growing fear.

He glanced at his dataplate. Work shift in less than two hours. He needed to leave, to blend in, to act normal. Normalcy was currency in Aethelburg, and he was currently bankrupt.

He picked up a discarded synth-fabric cleaning cloth, a cheap, grey square, and meticulously wiped up the spilled water. This time, he didn't try to move it with his mind. He didn't dare. He shoved the damp cloth into the recycling chute, then stared at his reflection in the polished surface of the re-fresher door. His face was pale, eyes wide and bloodshot. He looked like a man haunted.

"You're just tired," he murmured to his reflection, the words hollow. "You're imagining things."

But a spark, a tiny flicker of blue light, leaped from his reflection to his own face, a phantom kiss on his cheek. It wasn't imagined.

He quickly dressed in his standard-issue grey jumpsuit, the fabric coarse against his skin. He affixed his biometric identifier to his wrist, its faint green glow assuring him of his compliance. He took a deep breath, trying to steady his nerves, to push the hum and the impossible events of the last hour to the back of his mind. He had to go to work. He had to be Kael-713, efficient, compliant, invisible.

***

The transport tube hissed as it arrived at his sector station, disgorging a stream of identically clad workers. Kael merged with the flow, his head bowed, his gaze fixed on the scuffed synth-concrete floor. The hum of the city outside the tube was a cacophony now, not just a single drone, but a symphony of vibrations. He could feel the low thrum of the ground-transport vehicles above, the high-pitched whine of the ventilation shafts, the rhythmic pulse of the power conduits embedded in the walls. It was overwhelming.

He walked past the ubiquitous surveillance nodes, their unblinking red eyes scanning every citizen. Usually, he felt nothing but a vague sense of unease. Today, he felt their energy, a distinct ripple in the current, like individual nodes in a vast, predatory web. He felt them *touching* him, probing, not just seeing. He instinctively pulled his own internal hum tighter, making it smaller, less noticeable. He wasn’t sure why or how, but it felt like a crucial act of concealment.

The air in the lower levels of Aethelburg, where the factories and processing plants churned, was thick with the metallic tang of chemical solvents and recycled exhaust. It scraped at Kael’s throat, but now, his senses heightened, he could discern individual components: the sharp ammonia from waste processing, the cloying sweetness of synth-nutrient paste, the faint, acrid scent of ozone from the atmospheric scrubbers. It was too much. His head pounded.

He needed something to anchor him, something familiar and mundane. His job. Electro-processing. Sorting raw energy conduits, ensuring their integrity, routing them to the correct output. Mindless, repetitive work that usually numbed his senses.

He reached his station, a cramped cubicle with a single glowing console. His supervisor, a drone-like figure named Overseer Thorne, a man whose face was perpetually set in a frown of mild irritation, was already there.

"713," Thorne grunted, not looking up from his datapad. "Late by point-zero-zero-five cycles. Your compliance rating has been noted."

Kael felt a flush of heat rise up his neck. "Apologies, Overseer. Internal systems recalibration." A standard, vague excuse that usually sufficed.

Thorne merely scoffed, his gaze still fixed on his datapad. "Ensure it doesn't happen again. Productivity is paramount." He finally looked up, his eyes, dark and flat, sweeping over Kael. "You seem… agitated, 713. Is there a problem?"

Kael's heart seized. He felt the surveillance nodes' energy intensify as Thorne spoke, focusing on him. He felt the hum in his head pulse erratically. He had to be calm.

"No, Overseer," Kael said, forcing his voice to be level, devoid of emotion. "Just anticipating today's efficiency quotas."

Thorne's gaze lingered for another moment, then, finding nothing overtly amiss in Kael's carefully constructed neutrality, he returned to his datapad. "See that you meet them."

Kael slipped into his cubicle, the synth-steel walls seeming to press in on him. He activated his console. A stream of raw energy conduits, glowing faintly with contained power, began to flow past his station on a conveyor belt. His task was to scan each one, verify its integrity, and direct it.

He picked up his hand scanner. His fingers felt clumsy, thick. As he brought the scanner to the first conduit, the hum in his head flared. He saw not just the conduit, but its underlying energy field, a vibrant, complex web of currents and fluxes, like a living thing. He saw a tiny fracture, invisible to the naked eye, a hairline flaw that the scanner wouldn't register for another hundred cycles.

He instinctively reached out, not with his hand, but with that unseen current, that internal hum. He felt a pressure building in his skull, focused on the flaw. He wasn't trying to fix it, just… feel it. Explore it.

And then, impossibly, the conduit pulsed. The faint glow intensified, then dimmed. The hairline fracture, for a fleeting moment, seemed to shimmer, then vanish.

Kael's breath hitched. He pulled his hand back as if burned. He stared at the conduit. It looked perfectly fine. The internal hum receded slightly, as if exhausted.

He brought the scanner to it. *Integrity: 100%.*

He felt a wave of nausea. He hadn't just *seen* the flaw; he had *interacted* with it. He had… repaired it? No, that was insane. He must be hallucinating. The stress, the lack of sleep. This was all a delusion.

But another conduit approached, and he felt the urge again. This time, he didn't try to repair it. He just focused, let the hum guide him. He saw another flaw, a different kind, a subtle instability in the energy flow. He didn't touch it with his mind, just watched it pass. When he scanned it, the console flashed: *Integrity: 98%. Flagged for reprocessing.*

His ability wasn't just to move things. It was to *see* things differently, to perceive the hidden energies, the underlying currents that animated the physical world. And, terrifyingly, to *influence* them.

He worked through the shift in a daze, his compliance rating plummeting as his efficiency suffered. He kept seeing the flaws, the subtle imperfections in everything – the synth-steel walls, the very air, even the distant, bored expressions on his fellow workers' faces. He felt the undercurrents of their emotions, a dull hum of resignation, a sharp pang of envy, a faint throb of fear. The world was alive with energy, and he was suddenly attuned to its every pulse.

He kept his head down, desperately trying to ignore the constant flood of sensory data. He felt like a raw nerve exposed to the elements. Every sound was too loud, every light too bright, every surface vibrating with unseen power.

He managed to perform his duties well enough to avoid direct reprimand, though Thorne's irritated glances grew more frequent. The thought of being sent for 'recalibration' or worse, 'reprocessing', hung heavy in the air, a constant threat.

As the shift ended, Kael fled the plant, barely acknowledging the weary nods of his co-workers. He needed solitude. He needed to understand.

***

Back in the sterile confines of his hab-unit, the manufactured hum of Aethelburg felt less oppressive, now that he was actively trying to understand his connection to it. He locked the door, a flimsy barrier against a world that now felt full of threats.

He sat on his cot, cross-legged, and closed his eyes. He focused on the hum. It was no longer a distant tremor but a vital part of him, an extension of his own consciousness. He felt it emanating from within, but also stretching outwards, connecting him to the unseen currents of the city itself.

He held out his hand. He looked at the water ration cube he had failed to clean up perfectly earlier; a single, small bead of water remained on the synth-mat. He concentrated, willing it to rise. He felt the hum surge, felt it flow from his mind, down his arm, through his fingers, towards the water. It was a pressure, a tension, like pulling a taut string.

The water bead shivered. Then, slowly, infinitesimally, it began to levitate.

Kael gasped, his eyes wide. It was real. It was undeniably real. The bead floated an inch above the surface, shimmering in the dull light. He held it there, his whole body trembling with effort and disbelief. The hum in his head was a high-pitched whine now, demanding, draining.

He tried to move it, to steer it. The bead wobbled, drifted erratically, then with a sudden burst of power, shot across the room and splattered against the wall.

He collapsed back onto his cot, panting, exhausted. The hum receded, leaving him feeling hollowed out, drained. The surge of power, the raw, unrefined energy, had been exhilarating and terrifying. It was like learning to breathe for the first time, but into an atmosphere of pure, untamed force.

He spent the next hours experimenting, cautiously. He learned to levitate small objects – a discarded data-chip, a synth-fibre button, a speck of dust. He discovered that control was fleeting, his focus constantly breaking. Sometimes the objects would shoot off wildly, other times they'd simply refuse to budge. He felt the hum like a muscle, flexing and tiring. It was connected to his emotions too; fear and frustration made it wild and unpredictable, while a sense of calm focus seemed to give him a measure of control, however brief.

He discovered another facet of his awakening. He could manipulate the hum not just as a force, but as a sense. He could extend his awareness outwards, feeling the subtle vibrations of objects beyond his line of sight. He closed his eyes and pushed his awareness into the adjacent hab-unit. He felt a faint, rhythmic vibration – someone sleeping, their heart beating steadily. He felt the low hum of the ventilation system, the faint, internal shudder of the building itself.

It was like having a thousand new eyes, a thousand new ears, all tuned to the raw energy of existence. It was overwhelming, but within the chaos, he began to discern patterns, rhythms. The world was no longer just solid matter; it was a symphony of unseen currents, and he, impossibly, was a conductor.

But what did it mean? Was he alone? Were there others? The thought was both exhilarating and terrifying. If Aethelburg discovered him, they would dissect him, reprocess him. He couldn't risk exposure. He had to learn to control this, to hide it.

As the synthetic dusk settled outside, painting the sky in artificial oranges and purples from the atmospheric processors, Kael sat in the dim light of his hab-unit, a single levitating button orbiting his finger like a tiny moon. The hum was less a roaring in his head, more a steady, warm presence now, a part of him.

He looked at his reflection in the re-fresher door again. This time, his eyes held a new light, a flicker of something defiant, something *awake*. He wasn't just Kael-713 anymore. He was something more, something dangerous, something with a nascent power that could shatter the carefully constructed reality of Aethelburg.

He let the button fall, catching it in his palm. The weight felt solid, reassuring. He was changing. The world was changing.

He remembered a story from his childhood, a forbidden tale whispered by his grandmother before she was re-educated. A myth of the 'Ancients' who could manipulate the very fabric of existence, who lived in harmony with the world's natural pulse. He'd dismissed it as nonsense, childish fantasy. Now…

He focused on the hum again, letting it resonate through him. He reached out with his mind, not just to the adjacent hab-unit, but further, deeper, past the walls, past the layers of synth-steel and concrete, towards something… else. A faint, almost imperceptible tremor, a different kind of hum, pulsed beneath the city's drone. It was weaker, wilder, more organic. And it felt like it was calling to him, a faint, almost silent whisper beneath the roaring din of Aethelburg. A whisper from *outside*.

He didn't know what it was, or where it came from, but it felt… familiar. And it felt like hope. A dark, dangerous hope that might lead him to something far beyond the steel walls of his prison. He had to find out what that hum was, and why it felt like a part of him.

He stood, a new resolve hardening his jaw. He knew he couldn't stay hidden forever, not with this power surging through him. He had to move. He had to seek. And as he did, he realized the faint whisper wasn't just a general call. It was a directed impulse, a faint, almost subliminal map forming in his mind, pointing him towards the forbidden, forgotten lower sectors, towards the very heart of the city’s oldest, darkest secrets.
