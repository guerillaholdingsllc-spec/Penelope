## Protecting Your Clients in the Quantum Future

The way we protect client data is changing. Preparing for the quantum future ensures client data safety for years to come.

## What Is NPPI — And Why the Quantum Future Threatens It

NPPI stands for Non-Public Personal Information. This term also includes NPI. It describes sensitive client records. Think names, addresses, health details, or financial accounts. This information must be kept private.

Most client records are currently protected by strong encryption. Encryption (the technology that scrambles your data so only you can read it) keeps it safe. Today's encryption relies on complex math problems. Current computers cannot solve these problems quickly. However, powerful new computers are coming. These are called quantum computers. They can solve these math problems very fast.

Experts believe quantum computers could break current encryption. This could happen between 2030 and 2035. Bad actors know this. They might be stealing encrypted client records today. This is called "harvest now, decrypt later." They plan to decrypt (unlock) it later. This puts your long-held client records at risk. Protecting NPPI is crucial for your business.

## The 5-Step Client Data Safety Checklist

Here is your simple checklist for the quantum future.

### ☐ Step 1: Know What Client Data You Hold

You cannot protect what you don't know you have. List all places where you store client records. This includes physical files, computer servers, and cloud services. Identify any NPPI in these locations. Know how long you need to keep it. Some client records need to be kept for many years. These long-held records are most vulnerable to "harvest now, decrypt later." Regularly review your data storage practices.

### ☐ Step 2: Ask Your IT Provider One Simple Question

Your IT provider is a key partner. Ask them about quantum-safe security. Specifically, inquire about NIST's new standards. NIST published three new security standards. These are FIPS 203, FIPS 204, and FIPS 205. They came out in August 2024. These replace older RSA and ECC encryption. This is the technology protecting most websites today. Ask about their plan to update your systems. Your IT provider should understand these changes. They can guide your migration. Remember, US government agencies must migrate by 2035. Canada's federal deadline for migration PLANS is April 2026.

### ☐ Step 3: Check Your Software Vendors' Plans

Many businesses use specialized software. Think of practice management systems or accounting software. These vendors also protect your client records. Ask them about their quantum-safe plans. When will their systems support the new NIST standards? Google started testing quantum-safe security in Chrome in 2023. This shows it is already happening. Your vendors should be working on this too. Staying updated with vendors is vital.

### ☐ Step 4: Protect Your Long-Stored Client Records Now

This step is critical for older client records. Even if encrypted, they are at risk later. Consider re-encrypting very sensitive, long-term data. Use the new NIST-approved algorithms if available. This protects against "harvest now, decrypt later" threats. Consult your IT provider for this specialized task. Proactive protection secures your future.

### ☐ Step 5: Document Your Business Continuity Plan

A good plan ensures your business keeps running. What if your data systems were compromised? How would you continue serving clients? Think about backup and recovery strategies. Make sure your backups are also quantum-safe. This supports your overall client data safety. Regularly review and update your plan. A strong plan maintains business continuity.

New quantum-safe standards will impact laws. Existing laws focus on client data safety. Think HIPAA for medical and dental practices. GLBA (Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act) impacts financial firms. State privacy laws, like CCPA, also apply. These laws will adapt to the quantum era. They will likely require using new encryption standards. US government agencies must migrate by 2035. Commercial entities will follow soon after. Understanding these changes helps you stay compliant. Proactive steps protect you from penalties. It ensures trust with your regulators.

## 6 Plain-English Terms Your IT Person Will Use

*   **Encryption:** This scrambles your data, so only authorized people can read it.
*   **Decryption:** This is the process of unscrambling encrypted data back to its original form.
*   **Quantum Computer:** A new type of computer, still in development, that can solve complex problems very quickly.
*   **Algorithm:** A step-by-step set of rules or instructions used by a computer to solve a problem.
*   **Migration:** The process of moving from older security technologies to newer, quantum-safe ones.
*   **NIST Standards:** Guidelines published by the US government for how to protect data securely.
