## The 30-Second Version
"Quantum computing" (a new type of computer) is emerging. It handles information very differently. These powerful new computers are not yet common. They could break today's internet security (how we keep digital data safe). Security experts expect this threat by 2030-2035.

However, some data is already at risk. Bad actors (criminals or spies) are now stealing sensitive information. They plan to unlock it later. This is called "harvest now, decrypt later." New security standards are available. NIST (a US government standards body) released them in 2024. Many organizations are now updating their systems. It is wise to prepare for this change.

## Why You Are Hearing About This Now
"Quantum computing" was once science fiction. Now it is becoming a reality. Scientists have made great progress. Google, Microsoft, and Amazon are investing much money. They are already updating their cloud services (online data storage).

Today's data security (digital locks) uses complex math. Quantum computers could solve this math very quickly. This could make current digital locks simple to pick. This major threat is not for today. It is predicted for the next 5 to 10 years. But updating security takes much time. Changing large systems is a big job.

NIST released new security standards in 2024. These are for "post-quantum cryptography" (new, stronger digital locks). These new locks will protect against quantum attacks. Government agencies are already starting this work. US agencies plan to update by 2035. Canadian federal departments had an April 2026 planning deadline. This deadline was for starting their system updates. This shows the importance for many groups.

## Is Your Data at Risk?
Yes, some data faces a risk today. This is the "harvest now, decrypt later" danger. Bad actors are collecting encrypted data. "Encrypted data" means information that is scrambled. They cannot read it at this moment. But they are saving it for later. They are waiting for powerful quantum computers. Then they will use these to unlock the information.

Think of it like stealing a locked safe. You cannot open it today. But you might find a new tool later. Data that must be kept long is most at risk. This includes healthcare records. Financial transactions also face danger. Government secrets are another key target. HR data, like employee numbers, is also exposed. Long-term contracts are a significant concern. Many organizations store data for many decades. This information could be readable by 2030-2035. Data kept over 5 years is a target today.

## What Your Vendors Are Already Doing
Major cloud providers (companies offering online data storage) are preparing. Google is now updating its services. Microsoft is also making changes. Amazon is actively working on this too. They are building "quantum-safe" systems. "Quantum-safe" means able to resist quantum attacks. These systems follow the new NIST standards.

They are changing their backend (hidden) infrastructure. This affects how your data is stored. It also impacts how information moves. Many software vendors are doing similar work. They are updating their business applications. Their goal is to avoid the future threat. This work is often not visible to users. It happens behind the scenes. These vendors are starting their updates early. They understand the long planning timeline. Security experts suggest acting sooner rather than later. Many organizations are choosing to start now.

## 6 Terms Your Board Will Use (plain English definitions)
1.  **Quantum Computing:** This is a new type of computer. It uses different rules than today's machines. It can solve some very hard problems quickly.
2.  **Cryptography:** This is the science of secret codes. It keeps digital data safe. It scrambles data so only authorized people can read it.
3.  **Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC):** These are new digital security methods. They are designed to be safe from quantum computers. NIST recommends these new standards.
4.  **Harvest Now, Decrypt Later (HNDL):** This describes a current threat. Bad actors steal encrypted data today. They save it. They will unlock it later with quantum computers.
5.  **NIST (National Institute of Standards and Technology):** This is a US government agency. It sets many technology standards. NIST recommends new security methods.
6.  **Migration:** This is the process of changing systems. For example, moving from old security to new. This is often a big, multi-year project.
