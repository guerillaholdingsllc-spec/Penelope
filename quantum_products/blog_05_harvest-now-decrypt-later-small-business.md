## What is 'Harvest Now, Decrypt Later'? A Hidden Danger

"Harvest now, decrypt later" (HNDL) describes a specific type of cyberattack. Malicious actors steal encrypted data today. They store it for future decryption. They are betting on technological advances to break current encryption.

Current encryption methods are strong. They protect data effectively against today's computers. However, quantum computers promise immense processing power. This power could render today's best encryption useless.

Attackers are playing a long game. They collect vast amounts of encrypted client records. They wait for the "quantum future" to reveal all secrets. This makes client data safety a long-term challenge.

## Your Client Records: A Long-Term Target

Many small businesses handle sensitive information. This includes Personally Identifiable Information (PII) like names and addresses. It also often includes Non-Public Personal Information (NPPI) or Non-Public Information (NPI). NPPI refers to private data collected by financial institutions. NPI is the healthcare equivalent for patient data.

This type of information has long-term value. It can be used for identity theft, fraud, or competitive advantage. Even old data can be valuable years later. Your client records are a treasure trove for these patient attackers.

Existing security measures protect against immediate threats. But they don't guard against tomorrow's decryption capabilities. This creates a critical vulnerability for your most valuable assets. Your client records are at continuous risk.

## The Approaching Quantum Future: Opportunity and Responsibility

The quantum future is not just a threat; it's also an opportunity. It represents a new era of computing. This era will bring incredible advancements. It also brings the responsibility to secure our digital world.

Experts predict a realistic quantum threat to encryption by 2030-2035. This timeline gives businesses a window to prepare. It means that the data harvested today will be vulnerable within the next decade.

Leading organizations are already reacting. Google has been testing quantum-safe Chrome since 2023. This shows the industry's awareness. It also highlights the need for new security standards.

The National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) is leading this effort. In August 2024, NIST published three new quantum-safe standards: FIPS 203, FIPS 204, and FIPS 205. These standards define algorithms resistant to quantum attacks. They provide a roadmap for future data protection.

For small businesses, this is a call to action. Proactive steps taken today will protect your client data safety tomorrow. Embracing these changes is part of your responsibility to clients.
