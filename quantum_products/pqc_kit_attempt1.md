## Protecting Your Clients in the Quantum Future

The "quantum future" is coming. It brings both opportunities and new challenges for client data safety. Preparing now safeguards your client records and strengthens your business.

## What Is NPPI — And Why the Quantum Future Threatens It

NPPI (Non-Public Personal Information) or NPI (Non-public Personal Information) is sensitive client data you collect. This includes names, addresses, health records, or financial details. Think of it as any private information you keep about your clients. Protecting this information is crucial for your reputation and your clients' trust.

Currently, strong encryption (the technology that scrambles your data so only you can read it) keeps NPPI safe. Most websites and systems use encryption like RSA and ECC. However, extremely powerful future "quantum computers" could easily break this current encryption. This means client records you protect today could be at risk tomorrow.

Bad actors (people who want to steal data) know this. They might steal your encrypted client records now. They would store them, waiting to decrypt them later when quantum computers are powerful enough. This is called "harvest now, decrypt later." Experts realistically expect quantum computers to break current encryption between 2030 and 2035. The good news is that new, quantum-safe encryption standards are being developed and adopted.

## The 5-Step Client Data Safety Checklist

### ☐ Step 1: Know What Client Data You Hold

You cannot protect what you do not know you have. Take inventory of all your client records. Where are they stored? This includes paper files, computers, cloud services, and backup drives. Identify all NPPI you collect and how long you keep it. Understanding your data landscape is the first critical step for client data safety.

### ☐ Step 2: Ask Your IT Provider One Simple Question

Talk to your IT support or managed service provider (MSP). Ask them: "What is our plan for quantum-safe client data safety?" Your provider should be aware of new encryption standards. NIST published three new security standards in August 2024: FIPS 203, FIPS 204, and FIPS 205. These new standards replace the older RSA and ECC encryption. Your IT team should be researching how to implement these changes.

### ☐ Step 3: Check Your Software Vendors' Plans

Contact all your key software vendors. This includes your Electronic Health Records (EHR) system, accounting software, and client management tools. Ask them about their "quantum readiness" plans. Are they adopting the new FIPS 203, FIPS 204, and FIPS 205 standards? Google started testing quantum-safe security in Chrome browser in 2023. This shows that major tech companies are already preparing. Ensure your vendors are too.

### ☐ Step 4: Protect Your Long-Stored Client Records Now

The "harvest now, decrypt later" threat is real. Any client records you store for many years are especially vulnerable. This includes old patient charts or financial statements. Consider re-encrypting this historical NPPI using the new quantum-safe standards. Work with your IT provider on this important step. US government agencies must be fully migrated to new standards by 2035. Canada's federal deadline for migration PLANS is April 2026. This means preparations need to start soon.

### ☐ Step 5: Document Your Business Continuity Plan

A strong business continuity plan ensures you can keep operating even if there is a data incident. This includes recovering client records safely. Update your plan to address potential quantum threats. How would you handle a breach of old, encrypted client data? Think about secure backups and recovery procedures. This planning helps maintain client trust and minimizes disruption.

## What "Quantum Future Ready" Means for Your Business

Being "quantum future ready" means proactively adopting new data safety measures. It is more than just protecting against threats. It is a business opportunity. Businesses that prepare will gain a significant competitive advantage. They will show leadership in client data safety. This readiness also ensures smoother regulatory compliance as new rules emerge. You will build stronger trust with your clients.

## Regulatory Compliance: What's Coming

New regulations will certainly follow the adoption of quantum-safe encryption. Existing laws like HIPAA (for medical practices) already mandate strong data protection. GLBA (Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act for financial institutions) also requires safeguarding client financial information. State privacy laws (like CCPA in California) are also evolving. Future updates to these laws will likely require quantum-safe encryption. Preparing now ensures your future regulatory compliance. It helps you avoid penalties and maintain your reputation.

## 6 Plain-English Terms Your IT Person Will Use

1.  **Encryption:** This is the process of scrambling your data to protect it from unauthorized access.
2.  **Quantum Computer:** A new type of computer far more powerful than today's machines, capable of solving very complex problems.
3.  **RSA/ECC:** These are older, widely used encryption methods that quantum computers will eventually be able to break.
4.  **FIPS Standards:** These are new US government security standards for quantum-safe encryption, published by NIST.
5.  **Migration Plan:** This is a roadmap for moving from old encryption methods to new, quantum-safe ones.
6.  **Data Breach:** This occurs when unauthorized individuals gain access to your client records.
