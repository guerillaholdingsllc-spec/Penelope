## What Is This About?
Future super-fast computers are coming. These are called quantum computers. They will break today's internet security. New security is being developed now. This guide helps your small business prepare.

## Why Your Business Should Know About This
Your business uses many digital tools. These include online payment systems. Your website is also important. Email uses digital security too. Many systems use **encryption** (scrambling data so only authorized users can read it). This protects your customer records. It keeps your business safe. New computers could break this protection. This guide will help you understand the risks. It will show you simple steps to take.

## The Threat in Simple Terms
Scientists are building quantum computers. These machines will be very powerful. Current **encryption** (scrambling data so only authorized users can read it) protects your data. It uses complex math problems. Quantum computers can solve these problems quickly. This means they can break today's security. This could happen as early as 2030. It might even be by 2035. Attackers might steal your encrypted data now. They will wait for quantum computers. Then they will decrypt it later. This is called "harvest now, decrypt later." Your customer records could be exposed. Your financial data could become public. Your business needs new defenses.

**NIST** (the US National Institute of Standards and Technology) is helping. They are developing new security. These new standards protect against quantum threats. They published FIPS 203 this year. FIPS 204 and FIPS 205 also came out in 2024. These are the new security rules. US government agencies must use them. They need to switch by 2035. Many businesses are also preparing. Google started testing new security in Chrome in 2023. This shows the change is already starting.

## Your 5-Step Checklist

### Step 1: Learn About Your Current Systems
Understand what technology your business uses. Think about your payment systems. Consider your website's security. How do you store customer records? Do you use cloud services? Many systems use **cryptography** (the science of locking data so only the right person can read it). List all your important digital tools. Write down which vendors provide them. Make a simple inventory. This is your starting point.

### Step 2: Talk to Your IT Provider
Connect with your IT support team. If you have a small IT vendor, reach out. Ask them about post-quantum **cryptography** (the science of locking data so only the right person can read it). Use simple language. Ask if they know about the new NIST standards. These are FIPS 203, FIPS 204, and FIPS 205. Ask about the quantum threat. They can help you understand your risks. They can explain how your systems use encryption. This is an important conversation. Security experts suggest this first step.

### Step 3: Ask Your Key Vendors About Their Plans
You use many different software tools. Your email provider is one example. Your accounting software is another. Payment processors are very important. NIST recommends you contact these vendors. Ask about their post-quantum plans. Do they know about the NIST standards? When will their systems be updated? How will they protect your customer records? This is a growing concern. Many businesses are choosing to ask these questions. Keep a record of their answers.

### Step 4: Check Your Website's Security
Your website's security is very important. It protects your visitors. It keeps your own data safe. Many websites use **encryption** (scrambling data so only authorized users can read it). This encryption protects your customers' information. Ask your web host or developer about updates. Do they plan to upgrade your website's security? Will they use quantum-safe standards? Google is already testing this in Chrome. This shows the importance of web security.

### Step 5: Plan for Future Changes
This shift will not happen overnight. It will take time. NIST recommends creating a plan. This plan does not need to be complex. Just consider future security needs. Think about your data. Which data is most sensitive? How long do you keep it? "Harvest now, decrypt later" is a real threat. Sensitive data stolen today could be exposed in 2030. Many businesses are choosing to assess their long-term data risks. Discuss this with your IT team. Plan for upcoming security upgrades.

## 6 Words You Might Hear
1.  **Quantum Computer:** A new, very powerful computer. It can solve problems today's computers cannot.
2.  **Algorithm:** A set of rules or steps. Computers follow these steps to do a task.
3.  **Migration:** The process of moving from old systems to new ones. Here, it means switching to quantum-safe security.
4.  **PQC:** Stands for "Post-Quantum Cryptography." This is the new security designed for quantum threats.
5.  **Digital Signature:** A way to prove who sent a digital message. It also shows the message was not changed.
6.  **Cyberattack:** An attempt to steal or damage data. It can also disrupt computer systems.
