## Quantum Computing in Plain English
A regular computer tries one code at a time. It works through options sequentially. This process is very fast for many tasks. A quantum computer can test many codes at once. It explores possibilities far more efficiently. This makes it much faster for certain complex problems. It does not try all possibilities simultaneously. It uses quantum effects to speed up specific calculations.

Think of a keyring. A regular locksmith tries one key at a time. They test each key in the lock. This takes time. Quantum computing allows trying many keys at the same time. This applies to certain types of locks. It speeds up finding the right key. This method is not universal. It only works for specific mathematical problems.

A regular coin is heads OR tails. It shows one state. A quantum bit, or qubit, is different. It can be both states at once. This "both states" condition is called superposition. It lasts until the qubit is measured. Measurement forces it into one state.

Two quantum bits can be linked. This link is called entanglement. Measuring one instantly tells about the other. Their fates are connected. This connection is immediate. However, this cannot send information faster

## Post-Quantum Cryptography — What It Means

NIST released three new security standards in 2024. These are called FIPS 203, FIPS 204, and FIPS 205.

*   **FIPS 203 (ML-KEM):** A new way to create and share secret keys for a lock. Quantum computers cannot pick this lock.
*   **FIPS 204 (ML-DSA):** A new type of digital signature, like an unforgeable key stamp. It cannot be forged, even by a quantum computer.
*   **FIPS 205 (SLH-DSA):** A backup signature method, an extra secure lock. Extra security if one standard is ever compromised.

**Cryptographic agility** is the ability to switch encryption methods easily. It is like updating software for your security systems.

## Glossary — 10 Key Terms
**Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC)**: New encryption methods that protect your data from future quantum computer attacks.
**Encryption**: The process of scrambling information so only authorized people can read it.
**Qubit (Quantum Bit)**: The basic unit of information in a quantum computer, which can be more complex than a regular computer bit.
**NIST**: A US government agency that creates technology standards, including those for cybersecurity.
**FIPS**: Government standards for how computer systems and encryption should work, especially for security.
**Harvest Now Decrypt Later**: A threat where hackers steal encrypted data today, planning to unlock it with future quantum computers.
**Cryptographic Agility**: The ability for an organization to quickly change its encryption methods when new ones are needed.
**Public Key Infrastructure (PKI)**: A system that uses digital keys to verify identities and secure online communications.
**Algorithm**: A step-by-step set of instructions that a computer follows to solve a problem.
**Zero-Trust Security**: A security idea where no one is trusted by default, and every access request must be verified.
